I've had a mild interest in stage tech, specifically lighting, since middle school where I was on the crew for a play. Fast forward to college, and I hear about a group of guys throwing an underground rave not far off campus. I checked it out, and it was amazing. They had a couple of static lights, but the DJs were talented and had no trouble hyping up the (pretty large) crowd.
That's when I decided to step back into lighting. I got in contact with the organizers, and they were happy to have me join.
### First Rave
Leading up to the first rave, I was looking for ways to control DMX fixtures with my laptop, and my search led me to a really interesting project called QLC+. It aims to be a robust, full-featured lighting controller while being completely free and open source, a perfect match for the Framework 13 it's being run on.
Next was I/O. Outputting signals to the fixtures was simple enough; an inexpensive adapter from Amazon was all it took. For input, I used my StreamDeck with the OpenDeck software to map MIDI messages to the buttons, which QLC+ could detect and use to trigger scenes.
I have a few DMX fixtures at home but didn't want to haul everything up, so for this first show I started small and bought four cheap DMX static pars.
When the rave rolled around, setup went smoothly and we ended up with a great-looking stage framed by tarps around our tables. The StreamDeck was a little limiting, but with BPM taps and some scene hotkeys, I put on a pretty good show.

### Second Rave
After the first rave I upgraded my setup with a much more feature-rich QLC+ workspace, an Akai APC Mini MIDI pad, and two $30 moving heads from the one and only AliExpress.
I revamped all of my scenes to include the movers, and during initial testing in my room we discovered they also had some (albeit simple) lasers built in.
For this rave, we had an independent sound technician bring in a speaker setup, including a big road case that I set all my gear up on.
Unfortunately, since all of the lights were on the table with the DJs and we had no crowd control barrier, they got knocked around throughout the night. The moving heads ended up out of sync, and somehow someone managed to accidentally switch one of them out of DMX control mode, which caused a lot of confusion until I went over to check on it in person.

### Third Rave
We went bigger for this one, mounting all six fixtures on a very MacGyver-ed truss rig. When I say MacGyver-ed, I mean it; the "truss" was a large tree branch we found, suspended from the bridge above with climbing rope. As DIY as it was, the setup worked perfectly, and the show was much better now that the crowd wasn't blocking or bumping into the lights.

### Moving Forward
I plan to keep doing more events and expanding my setup and knowledge.
Currently, I'm migrating all of my showfiles to QLC+ version 5, which has a much better operator experience.
I've also been thinking about building my own custom console, integrating the StreamDeck, Akai MIDI pad, one or two LCD screens, and possibly an onboard computer, something like the motherboard from a mini PC. If I end up doing that, I'll write another post about it.
